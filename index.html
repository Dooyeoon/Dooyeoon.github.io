About

I am a Ph.D. student in the Department of Artificial Intelligence at Ajou University. My research asks what it takes for large models to leave the benchmark and work under real constraints: limited compute, shifting data, and a physical world that will not wait for the next forward pass.

On the language side, I study how approximation error compounds with depth in LLMs, and how a layer-wise curriculum can contain it during distillation and pruning. On the embodied side, I build world-model vision-language-action (VLA) models that encode less of the visual past and recover what they drop through motion residuals, lowering training and inference cost while improving task performance. I am also drawn to neuro-symbolic architectures as a path toward robot policies that reason explicitly about the structure of a task.

Collaborations with a university hospital and a semiconductor inspection project keep me close to deployment, where models fail when the hospital, the imaging device, or the lighting changes. Making them hold up when it does is part of the work I care about most.

Research Interests

Efficient LLMs
Knowledge distillation, pruning, and curriculum-based compression, grounded in how error accumulates across layers.
Vision-Language-Action Models & World Models
Predictive VLA policies that are cheaper to train and run because they spend less compute on redundant visual input.
Robot Manipulation
Language- and vision-conditioned manipulation policies that fit tight compute budgets.
Neuro-symbolic Architectures
Combining learned perception with explicit, structured reasoning.
Robustness to Distribution Shift
Domain generalization across hospitals, imaging devices, and inspection conditions.
